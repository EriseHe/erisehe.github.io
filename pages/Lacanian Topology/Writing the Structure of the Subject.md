The obscurity cultivated in Lacan’s psychoanalysis often discourages readers. As Malcolm Bowie observes, “[Lacan] … contrives to suggest that a supreme obviousness is at work beneath the busy textures of his writing.” All too often, Lacan’s arguably awkward deployment of topology feels more obstructive than illuminating within his already elusive formulations.

This series of notes is dedicated to revisiting Lacanian psychoanalytic topology, drawing primarily on his late seminars and Will Greenshields’s _Writing the Structure of the Subject_. My aim is twofold: to introduce basic Lacanian ideas to those unfamiliar with his psychoanalysis, and to collect his foundational attempts to fuse topology with psychoanalytic theory “more than a metaphor.” In so doing, I hope to lay the groundwork for a proper topological framework that can serve computational purposes.

Lacanian psychoanalysis has long faced skepticism around his introduction of topology. Yet, after more than half a century of continuous developement field like TDA, and AI, we may have reached a threshold where his vision of assembling the _subject_ through the language of algebraic topology is no longer trivial, but pragmatic - perhaps even the only viable option if we ever seek to render his theory computationally accessible. Although Lacan’s original mathematization was certainly not intended for computation, the rigor and precision inherent in topology may offer keen insights for system‐level modeling of artificial subjectivity.

Since this project is not clinical but mathematical in orientation, these notes are not intended as a philosophical discussion. Rather, I strive for a clean, straightforward presentation of Lacanian psychoanalysis (acknowledging that some oversimplification of his theory is inevitable, but endurable for computational design).

Therefore, this collection serves as a **prerequisite** for transforming Lacan’s _structure of the subject_ into an essentially, if not fully, computable topological model.
